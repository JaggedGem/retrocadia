Sega Genesis

The sharp-edged 16-bit rival that made speed, attitude and a very loud startup sound part of the evening ritual.

Welcome to the next level

Sega's 16-bit machine arrived with a confident voice and a library built around momentum. Its blast-processing campaign was marketing, but the feeling was real: games moved fast, sounded bold and looked ready to break out of the television.

From Sonic the Hedgehog to Streets of Rage 2, the Genesis made its identity clear in the first few seconds of play. It was the console that treated speed as a personality.

Badge engineering carried the same hardware around the world under different names, but the sound — that ascending chime — stayed unmistakable.
