The polygon goes mainstream

When the PlayStation arrived in 1994, its compact discs offered more space for ambitious soundtracks, voice acting and experiments with three dimensions. The machine invited a broader audience into games without asking them to know the old rules first.

Its library moved comfortably from the haunted corridors of Resident Evil to the bright, strange planet of PaRappa the Rapper. That range was the point: the console made room for an entire new shelf of genres, from survival horror to rhythm action.

It also normalised a new ritual — the long load screen, the whirring drive and the small ceremony of swapping a disc.
