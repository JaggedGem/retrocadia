A careful kind of magic

Released in Japan as the Super Famicom in 1990, Nintendo's next home system refined the ideas that made its earlier hardware approachable. Its controller added shoulder buttons, its palette gave artists room to breathe, and its cartridge format made every new game feel like a new doorway.

From the quiet exploration of EarthBound to the kinetic precision of Super Metroid, the SNES became a machine for atmosphere as much as action. Composers treated its sound chip like an instrument, layering samples until a four-channel limit sounded like an orchestra.

It is the console most often named when people describe the moment games stopped being a novelty and started being a place you could return to.

The best hardware disappears into the experience. Archive note, 2024
